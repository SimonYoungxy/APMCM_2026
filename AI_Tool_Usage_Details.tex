\section*{2. Purpose and stages of use}

AI assistance was confined to two stages.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Implementation of the analysis pipeline.} The AI wrote and debugged the
Python scripts that read the three supplied data streams, ran the statistical procedures
and exported numeric results, figures and tables. The choice of which procedure to apply
at each step was made by the team and is documented in the paper's methods sections.
\item \textbf{Drafting of manuscript text.} The AI produced an initial English draft of
the narrative sections from the team's analysis outline. The draft was then revised by the
team, sentence by sentence, against the computed results.
\end{itemize}

AI was \textbf{not} used to invent data, references, numerical results or conclusions.
All ten statistical references are standard methodological sources (Kruskal--Wallis, Dunn,
Benjamini--Hochberg, random forests, Kaplan--Meier, Cox, AIC, events per variable); none
was generated by a language model.

\section*{3. Key interactions (representative)}

Interactions were iterative review loops rather than single prompts. Representative
instructions, paraphrased:

\begin{itemize}
\item ``Audit the supplied data files: report row counts, missing-value structure and any
variable that is missing as a deterministic function of another variable.'' This led to
identifying the second fundamental frequency as definitionally present only in two-voice
calls, and the pulse rate as absent for all flat calls.
\item ``Every inferential claim must be traceable to a value in the exported JSON. Flag any
number in the manuscript that cannot be traced.'' This produced the events-per-variable
budget and the explicit non-estimability statement for the approach behaviour.
\item ``Check whether the playback trials are independent, and if not, whether the analysis
accounts for it.'' This exposed that the 278 trials form 139 matched pairs, which led to a
change of method (see Section~4).
\item ``Verify every stated dimension against the raw data.'' This exposed a mismatch
between a stated experimental design and the 13 distinct playback distances actually
present.
\end{itemize}

\section*{4. Adopted content and human verification}

All AI output was reviewed by the team and corrected where wrong. Corrections made during
review include, but are not limited to:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Paired structure of the playback data.} The trials are 139 matched
natural-call/control pairs (identical date, context, distance and group size). The draft
used an independent two-sample test for the overall response rate. The team required a
matched analysis instead; McNemar's test on the paired table was added to the pipeline and
is now reported ($\chi^2=66.33$, $p=3.8\times10^{-16}$, paired odds ratio $37.0$), and the
assumption text was rewritten to state that pairing is available through the experiment
index and is used for this comparison. The regression models remain unclustered; the
revised assumption statement discloses this and the resulting anti-conservative standard
errors.
\item \textbf{Experimental design dimension.} A sentence describing future playback
experiments stated the wrong design size. Cross-checking against the raw data showed four
call-type levels (including the control) and thirteen playback distances, and the
statement was corrected.
\item \textbf{Multiple testing.} Per-measure significance was initially reported without
correction. A Benjamini--Hochberg correction across the six omnibus tests was added, and
the abstract and conclusions were aligned with the corrected result.
\item \textbf{Reproducibility.} Hard-coded absolute file paths were replaced by paths
derived from the script location, and the pipeline was re-run end to end to confirm that
all reported values reproduce.
\end{itemize}

integrity of the content of this paper, including all parts produced with AI assistance.
